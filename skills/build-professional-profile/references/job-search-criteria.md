# Job-search criteria

Create `JOB_SEARCH_CRITERIA.md` when the objective involves employment, founder matching, consulting, fractional work, opportunity discovery, or role comparison. Keep it separate from `PROFILE.md` because preferences and constraints change faster than career facts.

## Capture

- current objective and timing;
- prioritized role families or engagement types;
- required work content, authority, ownership, and team shape;
- company stage, product/domain, culture, and operating preferences;
- current commitments and how they affect availability;
- location, remote/hybrid/onsite, relocation, time-zone, and travel boundaries;
- work authorization and sponsorship constraints by market;
- compensation or founder financial requirements, if the person wants them recorded;
- positive ranking signals, deprioritization signals, and hard exclusions;
- unresolved hard constraints.

Distinguish `confirmed constraint`, `preference`, `working default`, and `unknown`. Do not infer hard constraints from a current location, past role, or public profile.

## Make it operational

Define an opportunity test that first rejects violations of confirmed hard constraints, then ranks viable opportunities using the person's real priorities. Prefer a short ordered rubric over a falsely precise weighted score unless the user requests scoring.

Titles are often weak proxies. Capture the actual work, authority, expected hands-on depth, product responsibility, people scope, and practical conditions that make an opportunity attractive.

If high-impact details such as work authorization, geography, compensation floor, or travel tolerance remain unknown, list them as questions. The dossier may still be used for broad discovery, but label those opportunities `needs confirmation` rather than actionable.
