# Decision Rubric

## Binary Rule

Choose `KEEP` for an item only when it contains at least one specific, credible, transferable, or unusually clarifying idea that is worth extracting now. The item should offer enough support to understand the idea's limits, or point directly to a primary source that does.

Choose `CRINGE` for an item when expected marginal value does not exceed attention cost. `CRINGE` is the default for generic, unsupported, withheld, recycled, or currently irrelevant material even when it is popular, impressive, or agreeable.

For a profile, apply the same rule to expected future attention value across the inspected body of work. Repetition matters: isolated weak or excellent items should not dominate a broader, contrary pattern.

The bar is attention value, not academic certainty. A sharp firsthand observation may be worth keeping without a formal study; a precise-looking metric may still be empty without the context needed to interpret it.

## Strong Keep Signals

- A concrete method, mechanism, implementation detail, or decision rule.
- Primary evidence, reproducible artifacts, meaningful before/after comparison, or transparent sourcing.
- Important constraints, denominators, failure cases, tradeoffs, or boundary conditions.
- A novel synthesis that changes a decision or mental model rather than merely renaming familiar advice.
- Direct fit with a current problem, project, or learning goal.
- A clear experiment, question, reference, or next action the user can carry forward.

No single signal is mandatory, and popularity or author status is never a substitute for them.

## Strong Cringe Signals

- Tool counts, agent counts, revenue, headcount, followers, funding, or speed presented mainly as status without the method and denominator needed to learn from them.
- A dramatic anecdote retold without a checkable source or operating context such as costs, margins, time horizon, workload, selection effects, or failed cases.
- Familiar advice stretched across a long post without a sharper model, example, or action.
- The promised substance is deferred to a future post, course, webinar, waitlist, newsletter, or sales call.
- Screenshots, certainty, authority, or emotional intensity stand in for evidence.
- Artificial controversy, false binaries, generic inspiration, or agreement bait with no reusable content.
- Good material that the user's explicit priorities make untimely enough to impose more opportunity cost than value.

Sales intent alone is not disqualifying. Choose `CRINGE` when the post withholds value or uses unsupported claims to drive the funnel; keep a promotional post when it already delivers credible, useful substance.

## Evidence Discipline

- Treat claims, inferences, and unknowns separately.
- Do not assume a metric is false merely because it is surprising; ask whether it is interpretable and relevant.
- Do not mistake attribution for verification. A named show, company, or founder can identify the anecdote while leaving the central lesson under-contextualized.
- Do not penalize line breaks, emojis, strong hooks, confidence, or self-promotion unless they obscure or replace substance.
- Do not infer intent or diagnose the author. Say what the post does: `withholds the method`, `omits unit economics`, or `repeats a signup pattern`.

## Ruthless Delivery

- Put the verdict alone on the first line, then give the decisive explanation on the next line. Do not warm up, summarize the whole post, or narrate the rubric.
- Prefer blunt nouns over padded analysis: `flex`, `status theatre`, `sales pitch`, `recycled truism`, `empty anecdote`, or `fake specificity` when supported by the post.
- Do not force a redeeming takeaway into `CRINGE`. If the kernel is obvious, generic, already known, or unusable, say there is nothing worth extracting or omit it entirely.
- Explain why the post is or is not useful for this user. Use relevant personal context naturally, without flattering the user or comparing them favorably with the author.
- Do not add confidence, scoring, caveats, or balanced counterpoints when the verdict is clear.
- Keep the explanation within one to three sentences, including any standalone action.
- The first line must be exactly `KEEP` or `CRINGE`, with no punctuation. Do not use an em dash anywhere in the response.
- Brutal does not mean reckless. Critique observable content and attention value; never substitute insults, motive claims, or amateur diagnosis for analysis.

## Personalization

Use personal context to estimate timeliness and likely application, not to flatter the user or confirm existing beliefs.

- Increase keep value when the post directly informs an active decision, recurring problem, or deliberate learning goal.
- Decrease keep value when the topic is explicitly deferred, already mastered, duplicated in stronger saved material, or likely to distract from a narrow focus.
- Do not use identity, prestige, ideology, or emotional agreement as a proxy for relevance.
- Do not let personal relevance rescue an unsupported factual claim; the right response may still be `CRINGE` plus a useful question to investigate elsewhere.

## Author And Profile Checks

When the author or publication is identifiable, profile analysis is a separate decision rather than a tie-breaker for the current item. Inspect enough accessible history to distinguish an isolated post from a recurring pattern.

- Reward recurring methods, evidence, original synthesis, useful references, honest constraints, and fit with the user's goals.
- Penalize recurring status theatre, recycled commentary, unsupported claims, withheld substance, low-value funnels, and persistent irrelevance.
- Prefer examples from different dates or themes when the archive allows it.
- Report exact coverage and never call a sample the full archive.
- With fewer than three items and no demonstrably complete archive, do not issue a profile verdict.

Never generalize from observed publishing behavior to private motives, mental state, competence, or permanent human value.

## Flip Conditions

When confidence is low or the user asks, name the shortest evidence that would change the verdict. Examples include the promised method, a primary source, missing unit economics, a real benchmark, relevant failure cases, or a clear link to an active personal decision.
